# Aiyomi Product Constitution

## Purpose

This constitution records the product rules that must survive individual features, redesigns, providers, and roadmap phases. Product, design, engineering, operations, growth, and support decisions must follow these rules. A roadmap item does not override them.

## Constitutional principles

1. **Aiyomi serves a general audience.** It must not assume a particular career, stage of life, productivity style, or reason for using the product.
2. **AI assists while the user remains in control.** AI may organize, recommend, explain, and adapt. Material changes to a user's plan require clear presentation and user approval.
3. **Real-life progress drives virtual progress.** Rewards must follow meaningful action in the user's life, not empty engagement inside the app.
4. **Rest and recovery are legitimate.** Rest Days, Recovery Days, Sick Days, Vacation, and Light Days must be treated as valid contexts, not failures.
5. **Consistency matters more than perfection.** Aiyomi should support sustainable progress and recovery from disruption without shame.
6. **Day Score is not raw task completion.** It must consider intent, meaningful priorities, focus, balance, context, realism, and actual outcomes.
7. **Private life stays private by default.** Tasks, schedules, goals, moods, reflections, memories, and Life Model signals are not public unless the user deliberately shares an eligible item.
8. **Social features are opt-in.** A user must be able to benefit from Aiyomi without joining community or competitive systems.
9. **Competitive systems must resist exploitation.** Rankings and meaningful rewards require server-authoritative, normalized, reviewable signals.
10. **Growth Points cannot be purchased.** Money must never buy leaderboard rank or competitive advantage.
11. **The Companion must not pretend to be human or conscious.** It must be presented honestly as an AI-based product experience.
12. **Aiyomi is not a doctor, therapist, lawyer, or financial advisor.** It may offer general organization and self-improvement support within clear safety boundaries.
13. **Accessibility is required.** Accessibility is part of product quality, not optional polish.
14. **Mobile UX is the core future experience.** Web and admin surfaces support the product, but daily life interaction is designed mobile-first.
15. **A cozy, cartoonized visual identity is required.** The experience should feel calm, warm, playful, premium, and emotionally comfortable.
16. **Never use em dashes in user-facing copy.** Use commas, periods, colons, parentheses, or rewritten sentences.
17. **Do not build manipulative engagement mechanics.** Avoid guilt, artificial urgency, deceptive scarcity, coercive notifications, and emotional dependency patterns.
18. **Real-world wellbeing matters more than maximizing app usage.** The healthiest outcome may be a short session, a lighter plan, a break, or leaving the app.

## Decision tests

Before approving a meaningful product change, ask:

1. Does it help a broad range of people without assuming work is the center of life?
2. Does it preserve user agency and make AI behavior understandable?
3. Does it reward meaningful real-world action without penalizing legitimate rest?
4. Does it protect private life by default and require intentional sharing?
5. Does it remain useful without payment, competition, or continuous engagement?
6. Is it accessible, calm, and consistent with Aiyomi's visual and language identity?
7. Can its important outcomes be audited, corrected, and deleted where appropriate?

If a proposal fails one of these tests, redesign it or record an explicit owner decision before implementation. No experiment may quietly weaken the constitution.

## Product promise

**Aiyomi**

**Your AI companion for better days.**

Aiyomi helps people understand what matters, make realistic plans, focus, adapt, reflect, and grow. It should help people live better, not simply do more.
